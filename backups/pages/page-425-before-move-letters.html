What does our Emerson Community mean to you?

This week’s personal testimonial is from Emerson member Bonsai:

“I grew up in a strict, conservative Christian environment, but I had an enjoyable experience in church growing up. As I got older, my beliefs changed considerably and I no longer felt comfortable at “regular” churches. I was happy to find Emerson because I had no idea that there could be a church where you can believe whatever resonates as truth for you and still come together and enjoy a spiritual community without a bunch of rules. Emerson satisfied my need for spiritual community without forcing me to adhere to a rigid set of beliefs. I appreciate that Emerson accepts everyone no matter who they are, what they believe, or where they’re at in life. It’s a very supportive and compassionate environment. (There are also a lot of neurodiverse people here so there is no pressure to act a certain way or pretend to be someone you’re not.) It’s a place where you can come as you are. Lastly, I appreciate how open Emerson is to everyone contributing their own gifts, talents, and ideas. In this way, it’s a great creative outlet where each person can bring a piece of themselves and use their unique gifts to serve the community.”

This week’s personal testimonial is from Emerson member Dave:

“We come to Emerson UU because it feels good to attend a place where the people there not only know our name but also subscribe to a set of principles and point of view of life including its spiritual aspects to which we subscribe.

As Emerson UU thus supports us, it only makes sense to support Emerson UU.

To ensure Emerson will be there we pledge hoping to help not only to keep the lights on but to help in the budgeting process to ensure the utilities and rent are paid and its religious services and mission objectives are met.”

This week’s personal testimonial is from Emerson member PJ:

“In my childhood, my parents forced me to attend the church that they were brought up in and both were not a community of support and build up others. As a teen, I met home schooled children in club soccer. They invited me to attend their demonstrative church - I didn't realize at the time - that veiled its control behind the Bible and personal beliefs of which did not resonate with me or that I found to be completely untrue and hateful. At an impasse, I turned my back on that church.

Even before I was finished with college, attending church nauseated me. I disliked attending even when friends invited me. After I moved to STL, I heard someone tell me they weren't religious and I adopted that because it fit well. Years later I met PJ and Mark at work. Separately they shared interesting things they would talk about at church. After hearing a coworker mention PJ was Neo-Pagan, I wanted to listen in. Coming to watch and support PJ'S winter solstice talk was invigorating. As I continued to attend and even into the launch of Zoom, I finally found the community I wanted all along. And it still took me some time to feel things out because of my inherent distrust with the church. But this isn't a church, it's a community where people want to help and support one another the best they can.”
