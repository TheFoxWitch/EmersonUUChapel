Emerson Chapel uses a few cookies, and one small note in your browser, so this website can work. We do not place advertising cookies ourselves, and we do not sell information about your visit. Some embedded services (for example YouTube or Google Maps) may still set their own cookies when you use them.

Sign-in cookies

When you sign in, WordPress stores cookies so you stay logged in. They last about two days, or about two weeks if you tick Remember Me. Logging out removes them. A short-lived test cookie checks that your browser accepts cookies at all. Member pages need these cookies. The site cannot keep you signed in without them.

Newsletter invitation

The homepage may show a pop-up asking you to join This Week at Emerson. If you close it or sign up, the browser saves a small note in local storage, which works like a cookie. The note is only “dismissed” or “subscribed,” plus the date. It has no name or email, and it is never sent to us. A closed invitation stays hidden for 30 days, then it can show again. After you subscribe, it stays hidden until you use Clear the newsletter reminder below, or clear this site’s data in your browser.

Newsletter emails

If you subscribe to This Week at Emerson, Mailchimp may use a small invisible image (a “tracking pixel”) and special links to tell us whether you opened a newsletter and which links you clicked. This works much like a cookie. It helps us see which messages are useful. You can unsubscribe at any time using the link in any newsletter, and you can turn off image loading in your email program to limit open tracking.

Third-party embeds

Some pages include YouTube, Zoom, PayPal, Microsoft Forms (our commitment form), Google Forms if an older link is used, or the Google Maps embed on Visit Us. Those services may set their own cookies when you use them. Videos from YouTube may set cookies when you play them. Their policies are linked from our Privacy Policy.

Your choices

You can block or delete cookies in your browser settings. If you block the sign-in cookies, member pages will not be able to keep you logged in.
